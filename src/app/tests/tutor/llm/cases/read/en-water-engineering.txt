Fictional Institute of Technology – School of Civil Engineering
CE 312 – Urban Water Systems
Spring term – 6 credits
Instructor: Prof. Jordan Lee (office hours Tuesday 2-4 pm)

Course description
This course covers the design and operation of the water systems that serve cities: drinking water supply, distribution networks, wastewater collection and treatment, and urban drainage. It puts a strong emphasis on how these systems can adapt to climate change and population growth. Students combine hydraulic calculations with the analysis of real network data.

Prerequisites: CE 210 Fluid Mechanics.

Learning outcomes
By the end of the course, students will be able to:
1. Size a drinking water distribution network for a given demand.
2. Explain the main stages of wastewater treatment and their purpose.
3. Estimate stormwater runoff with the rational method and design a simple drainage system.
4. Assess the resilience of an urban water system to drought and flooding.

Course outline
Week 1 – Introduction: the urban water cycle
Week 2 – Water demand and supply sources
Week 3 – Drinking water treatment
Week 4 – Distribution networks: hydraulics and pipe sizing
Week 5 – Network modelling with EPANET
Week 6 – Wastewater collection
Week 7 – Midterm exam
Week 8 – Wastewater treatment processes
Week 9 – Stormwater and urban drainage
Week 10 – Nature-based solutions: green roofs, swales and retention ponds
Week 11 – Water systems under climate change
Week 12 – Project presentations and review

Assessment
- Problem sets: 20%
- Midterm exam: 30%
- Group project (resilience study of a district water system): 50%

Readings
Selected chapters from a water engineering textbook and case studies posted on the course page.
